What it looks like

Screens from the app as it stands, taken on the Android TV emulator by the test suite on every push. The design mockups it grew from are in the repo.

Guide

Half-hour columns, a now-line, a preview card for the focused program, and stars on the channel column for favorites. Up and down follow the time column, OK tunes in.

Channels

Build a channel from shows, a whole library with genre and decade filters, a collection, or a playlist. Or let Clickarr suggest a starting lineup from what you own.

Household

Several TVs share one lineup and agree on the schedule over your own Wi-Fi, paired with a PIN over TLS. No extra server, no cloud account.

Player

Tune in and you are mid-episode, like television. The overlay shows the channel, the program, where you are in the slot, and what is up next.

Suggest channels

Clickarr reads your library once and proposes a starting lineup the way you would make it by hand: a rerun channel for each show you have the most of, your collections, the genres and decades you have plenty of, a shuffled movie channel, and a playlist. Untick what you do not want; nothing is created until you say so.

Setup

Sign in to Plex with a code on your phone, or type an address and token. Your sign-in stays on the TV.

How it's built

A single Android app that talks straight to your media server. The decisions that shape it:

1

Video goes server to TV

Clickarr never proxies media. Your server keeps doing direct play or transcoding; Clickarr asks for the right item at the right offset.

2

Schedules are math, not files

A channel is a function from time to program. Every TV computes the same answer from the same lineup, so nothing drifts and nothing needs syncing in real time.

3

Households, your way

TVs pair over the LAN with a PIN, one coordinating and the others caching the lineup. Or choose Google Drive sync instead, for backup and for TVs in different homes on the same Plex server. Planned.

4

Your tokens stay put

Each TV signs in to Plex itself. Household sync shares channels, never credentials.

5

Fire TV first-class

No Google Play Services anywhere. Sideload the APK on a Fire Stick and it just runs.

6

Open source, MIT

Clean-room, permissively licensed, built in public with architecture decisions written down.

In the spirit of the *arr apps

The name is a nod to Sonarr, Radarr, and the rest, and so is the attitude: self-hosted, open source, opinionated, and built to automate the tedious part so you can stop tending your library and start watching it. Clickarr suggests channels from what you own, keeps every TV on the same schedule without a server in the middle, and writes its decisions down in public. It is an independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by the Servarr team.
