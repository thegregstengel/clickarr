The other options kept missing the mark

Turning a Plex library into live channels is not a new idea. But the ways to do it tend to cost money every month, need a server or container running around the clock, put a cloud account between your TVs, or leave out the things that make it feel like TV. Clickarr was built to be the one without the catch.

What it looks like

Real screens, captured by the test suite on every build.

Guide

Two hours across, a now-line, a preview card with artwork and synopsis for the focused program, and stars on the channel column for favorites. Up and down follow the time column, OK tunes in.

Channels

Build a channel from shows, a whole library with genre and decade filters, a collection, or a playlist. In order or shuffled, with two or three episodes of a show in a row if you like, back to back or on the half hour.

Household

Several TVs share one lineup and agree on the schedule over your own Wi-Fi, paired with a PIN over TLS. No extra server, no cloud account. Or sync through Google Drive for TVs in different homes.

Player

Tune in and you are mid-episode, like television. The overlay shows the channel, the program, where you are in the slot, and what is up next.

Suggest channels

Clickarr reads your library once and proposes a starting lineup the way you would make it by hand: a rerun channel for each show you have the most of, your collections, the genres and decades you have plenty of, a shuffled movie channel, and a playlist. Untick what you do not want; nothing is created until you say so.

Setup

Sign in to Plex with a code on your phone, or type an address and token. Your sign-in stays on the TV, in its own Keystore, and is never synced.

Everything that was missing, in one app

The features that always seemed to be missing from the other options.

Channels your way

  • Build from shows, whole libraries with genre and decade filters, collections, or playlists.
  • In order or shuffled, and a shuffled channel can still play 2 or 3 episodes in a row, in aired order.
  • Back to back, or on 15, 30, or 60-minute slots with a "Coming up next" promo between programs: the next show's backdrop, time, and synopsis over its theme music.
  • Suggest channels builds a sensible starter lineup from your library.
  • New episodes join their channels on their own, or only when you press Refresh.
  • Seasonal channels: Halloween horror in October and Christmas movies in December, found in your library and back each year.

No mess in Plex

  • Tell Plex what you watched: off, mark watched, or full progress.
  • Mark watched only counts what you actually sat through. Tuning in for the ending does not count.
  • Your Continue Watching stays clean unless you choose full progress.

A real guide

  • A cable-style grid with a now-line, a preview card, and favorites.
  • A mini-guide while you watch, and number-key tuning.
  • Captions in your language: hold OK to switch them on, styled by your TV's caption settings.
  • Choose how far ahead it shows, 3 to 24 hours.
  • Hide the Favorites tab if you only have a handful of channels.

Make it yours

  • Themes: Clickarr navy, Dark, a slate Light, and Dracula.
  • Three interface sizes, so it fits any TV.
  • A clock with automatic network time and your time zone.
  • A settings lock code per TV, so nobody rearranges your channels.
  • A sleep timer and "Are you still watching?", so a channel left on overnight stops on its own.

Easy to keep running

  • In-app updates on the stable release or the nightly build, checked and installed from Settings, About.
  • Several TVs on one lineup: pair them over Wi-Fi, or sync through your Google Drive for TVs in different homes.
  • A Diagnostics screen with everything a bug report needs, and never your tokens.

Security, done properly

An app that holds your Plex sign-in should earn it.

1

Your token stays put

The Plex sign-in lives in the TV's Android Keystore. It never syncs to other TVs and never appears in logs or Diagnostics.

2

Pairing over TLS

TVs pair with a one-time PIN and pinned certificates, so a random device on your Wi-Fi cannot join your household.

3

The narrowest Drive access

Google Drive sync uses the smallest permission Google offers. Clickarr can see the one file it created, not the rest of your Drive.

4

Signed and reproducible

Every release is built twice in CI and checked to match, then signed. The signing fingerprint is published so you can verify the APK.

5

Nothing phones home

No analytics and no Clickarr servers. It talks to your Plex server, to GitHub when you check for updates, and to Google only if you turn on Drive sync.

6

Reviewed in public

The security review, including the known limitations, is written up in SECURITY.md.

In the spirit of the *arr apps

The name is a nod to Sonarr, Radarr, and the rest, and so is the attitude: self-hosted, open source, opinionated, and built to automate the tedious part so you can stop tending your library and start watching it. Clickarr suggests channels from what you own, keeps every TV on the same schedule without a server in the middle, and writes its decisions down in public. It is an independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by the Servarr team.

Get it

One APK. Sideload it once and it keeps itself up to date.

Fire TV

Install Downloader from the Amazon Appstore, then enter clickarr.net/apk.

Android TV & Google TV

Download the APK from GitHub Releases and install it with your favorite file manager or ADB.

Living on the edge

The daily build is at clickarr.net/nightly. Switch between release and nightly in Settings, About.
